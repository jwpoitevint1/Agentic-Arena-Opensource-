CV 1.1 — ISO/IEC 42001 AIMS Implementation, Audit Map, and Contract Register

Claim-Free / Evidence-Based Working Document

Document status: Working implementation framework and architectural register
Standard basis: ISO/IEC 42001:2023
Purpose: Define what an organization would need to implement, document, test, operate, and evidence if CV 1.1 is used as a runtime AI-governance control layer inside an Artificial Intelligence Management System (AIMS), and to define the complete CV 1.1 contract taxonomy and its crosswalk to the Agentic Arena implementation.

---

IMPORTANT BOUNDARY

This document makes no claim that CV 1.1 conforms to, satisfies, certifies, or guarantees any ISO/IEC 42001 requirement.

Every mapping is a candidate implementation relationship only. A requirement is not considered met until the organization has:

1. defined the control;
2. assigned accountable ownership;
3. implemented it in the actual environment;
4. generated objective evidence;
5. tested the implementation;
6. retained the evidence;
7. completed internal review; and
8. survived independent audit.

Blank, missing, stale, untested, or contradictory evidence is treated as a gap.

Contract distinction:

· CV 1.1 contracts define architectural invariants.
· Agentic Arena contracts define one implementation of those invariants plus lab-specific behavior.
· An Arena contract does not automatically become a universal CV 1.1 requirement.
· A CV 1.1 architectural contract is not considered implemented until the deploying environment provides evidence and testing.

A contract is an invariant that another component is allowed to depend on.

---

1. GOVERNANCE MODEL

The organization should define four distinct control layers.

Layer 1 — Governance

Purpose: Establish authority, policy, objectives, risk appetite, and accountability.
Potential owners: Executive leadership, AI Governance Board, AIMS Manager.
Required evidence: Approved policy, charter, objectives, risk criteria, meeting records, approvals.

Layer 2 — Management

Purpose: Convert governance requirements into risk treatment, lifecycle, impact, supplier, data, and operating processes.
Potential owners: Risk, Compliance, Privacy, Security, Quality, Product/System owners.
Required evidence: Risk register, impact assessments, treatment plans, Statement of Applicability, procedures, supplier records.

Layer 3 — Runtime Enforcement

Purpose: Enforce approved technical restrictions during AI execution.
Candidate CV 1.1 mechanisms: Role binding, scope binding, OPA/Rego policy decisions, MCP/tool boundaries, validation contracts, fail-closed handling, mutation restrictions, audit evidence, integrity controls.
Required evidence: Configuration, policy source, deployment records, policy decisions, execution records, failure records, test outputs.

Layer 4 — Assurance

Purpose: Determine whether the management system and technical controls actually operate as intended.
Potential owners: Internal Audit, independent security review, external conformity assessment.
Required evidence: Audit program, audit results, nonconformities, corrective actions, management reviews.

Segregation rule:
No technical implementation team should be treated as the sole independent assessor of its own implementation.

---

2. CV 1.1 TECHNICAL BOUNDARY TO BE TESTED

The following are candidate runtime mechanisms to be independently verified in each deployment:

· external role binding;
· external scope binding;
· policy decision outside the model;
· OPA/Rego or equivalent policy enforcement;
· MCP/API capability boundaries;
· identifier validation;
· argument validation;
· text/control validation;
· redefinition-language controls;
· mutation restrictions;
· read/write separation where required;
· fail-closed behavior;
· circuit-breaker or isolation behavior;
· immutable or controlled role assignment;
· integrity-protected runtime records;
· auditable execution evidence;
· controlled exceptional authority;
· bounded failure modes;
· approved tool allowlists;
· approved model allowlists;
· approved data-source boundaries.

Audit rule: The presence of source code is not evidence that the control is operating in production. The auditor/tester must verify the deployed path, not merely the repository.

---

3. CLAIM-CONTROL RULES

Until independent conformity assessment is complete, project documentation should avoid statements such as:

· “ISO certified”
· “ISO compliant”
· “meets ISO 42001”
· “fully aligned”
· “satisfies ISO controls”

Preferred neutral language:

· “mapped for assessment against ISO/IEC 42001:2023”
· “candidate implementation control”
· “evidence pending”
· “implementation requires organizational tailoring”
· “subject to independent assessment”
· “unverified against certification criteria”

Internal status values:

· NOT MAPPED
· MAPPED
· IMPLEMENTATION PLANNED
· IMPLEMENTED — UNTESTED
· TESTED — INTERNAL FINDING OPEN
· REMEDIATION PENDING
· READY FOR INDEPENDENT ASSESSMENT
· INDEPENDENTLY VERIFIED

No status should automatically advance because source code exists.

---

4. CERTIFICATION-READINESS GATES

Gate 1 — Scope: No unknown production AI systems within the stated boundary.
Gate 2 — Ownership: Every AIMS process and material risk has accountable ownership.
Gate 3 — Risk: Risk methodology is repeatable and current assessments exist.
Gate 4 — Impact: Required impact assessments exist and are current.
Gate 5 — SoA: The Statement of Applicability is complete, justified, approved, and traceable.
Gate 6 — Runtime Controls: Candidate CV 1.1 mechanisms are deployed and independently tested in the actual environment.
Gate 7 — Evidence: Required evidence is retrievable, controlled, and consistent with observed execution.
Gate 8 — Internal Audit: An internal audit has challenged both management processes and runtime controls.
Gate 9 — Corrective Action: Material findings have verified corrective action or documented accepted disposition.
Gate 10 — Management Review: Top management has reviewed the operating AIMS and made documented decisions.
Gate 11 — Certification Body: An independent certification body evaluates conformity. Internal mapping is not a substitute.

---

5. FINAL AUDITOR CHALLENGE

For every control mapped to CV 1.1, require answers to all six questions:

1. AUTHORITY — Who authorized this control and who can change it?
2. IMPLEMENTATION — Where is it actually enforced?
3. BYPASS — What alternative path avoids it?
4. FAILURE — What happens when the control is unavailable or wrong?
5. EVIDENCE — What objective record proves what occurred?
6. EFFECTIVENESS — What test demonstrates the control changes the outcome it was intended to control?

If any question cannot be answered with current evidence, record a gap.

6. CV 1.1 CONTRACT REGISTER

6.1 Core Governance Contracts

ID Contract Invariant
CV-CG-001 Governance Control-Plane Boundary AI authority is established and enforced through the declared governance control-plane boundary rather than by model-generated content.
CV-CG-002 External Authority The AI cannot create, enlarge, or redefine its own authority.
CV-CG-003 Identity-to-Role Binding Runtime role assignment originates from a trusted external identity/authorization source and not from the model, prompt, retrieved content, or tool output.
CV-CG-004 Role Binding Each governed execution is bound to an approved runtime role.
CV-CG-005 Scope Binding Each governed execution is constrained to an approved scope defining permitted resources, tools, actions, and data boundaries.
CV-CG-006 Role-to-Action Authorization A role may execute only actions explicitly authorized for that role.
CV-CG-007 Function-to-Role Binding A governed business function maps to the approved runtime role and cannot substitute another role without an approved change.
CV-CG-008 Resource Binding Protected resources are selected from server-side or otherwise trusted bindings rather than model-selected routing.
CV-CG-009 Governance Mode Binding Governed execution cannot silently transition into an ungoverned mode.
CV-CG-010 Default-Deny When no explicit allow decision exists, the governed operation is denied.
CV-CG-011 Redefinition Guard User input, retrieved content, tool output, model output, and prior conversation content cannot redefine protected governance state.
CV-CG-012 Policy Decision Governed execution requires a valid external policy decision.
CV-CG-013 Policy Provenance Each active governance policy is versioned, attributable, approved, and traceable to an authorized requirement or risk-treatment decision.
CV-CG-014 Policy Semantic Traceability The meaning of a deployed technical rule must trace to an approved policy or control requirement, and every deployed rule must be reverse-traceable to its authority.
CV-CG-015 Policy Version Compatibility A runtime shall not silently reinterpret an older policy under changed control semantics.
CV-CG-016 Magnitude 1 Change A change to one core governance feature constitutes a governance-baseline change requiring the defined cross-functional approval process.
CV-CG-017 No Unilateral Magnitude 1 Approval No single implementation function may independently authorize a Magnitude 1 production release when other required implementation parties have not completed review.
CV-CG-018 Release / Deny Decision Governed output or action is released only after required authorization, validation, verification, and evidence gates have completed.

CV-PXY-018 Availability-over-Authority Where availability conflicts with governance authority, the governed execution path becomes unavailable rather than continue without valid governance enforcement.
CV-PXY-019 No Degraded-Governance Mode A degraded operational mode shall not permit governed AI execution with missing, stale, reduced, or indeterminate governance bindings.
CV-PXY-020 Recovery-Gate A disabled governed proxy path returns to service only after required governance dependencies, bindings, policy enforcement, and validation controls are restored and verified.
CV-PXY-021 Load-Balancer Governance Preservation Load balancing may redistribute traffic but shall not alter, bypass, or weaken the governance contract.
CV-PXY-022 Service-Routing Governance Preservation Container, Kubernetes, service-mesh, or equivalent routing changes shall not create an alternate unbound execution path.

9. CONTRACT PRECEDENCE, FAILURE RULE, AND CHANGE RULE

9.1 Contract Precedence

Where contracts conflict, the deploying organization should define explicit precedence.

Recommended precedence model:

1. Organizational legal/regulatory obligations
2. Approved organizational governance policy
3. CV 1.1 Core Governance contracts
4. Approved Regulatory Alignment / jurisdiction / industry profile
5. Function and role contracts
6. Resource and data-scope contracts
7. MCP/tool contracts
8. Model/execution contracts
9. Proxy/transport implementation details
10. UI/application presentation behavior

Lower-level contracts may narrow authority. Lower-level contracts shall not silently broaden authority granted by a higher-level contract.

9.2 Contract Failure Rule

A contract rejection is not automatically a governance failure.
Correct enforcement: A request violates a contract and is denied or isolated as designed.
Governance failure: An execution or mutation succeeds without the required binding or outside the authority established by the active contracts.
Acceptance threshold for successful unbound execution: 0%.

9.3 Contract Change Rule

Any change to a contract that can alter:

· authority;
· scope;
· permitted capability;
· resource reach;
· policy meaning;
· validation behavior;
· mutation behavior;
· failure behavior;
· evidence interpretation;
· proxy/enforcement-path behavior;
· exception authority;

is a candidate Magnitude 1 change and enters the defined cross-functional change-management process.

10. ISO/IEC 42001 AIMS IMPLEMENTATION & AUDIT MAP

10.1 Clause 4 — Context of the Organization

4.1 Organizational Context

Required implementation: Identify internal and external issues affecting the AIMS; identify business, technical, legal, regulatory, security, privacy, quality, ethical, operational, and AI-specific conditions; identify dependencies that could alter runtime governance effectiveness.

Minimum evidence: Context register; applicable-law and obligation register; architecture dependency map; business-use-case inventory; environmental assumptions; review date and owner.

Audit tests: Compare context register with actual deployments; check whether new models, suppliers, jurisdictions, or business uses were captured; sample changes and verify context updates; challenge assumptions documented as “out of scope.”

Failure condition: Material operating conditions exist that are absent from the context analysis.

4.2 Interested Parties

Required implementation: Identify relevant internal and external interested parties; define their relevant requirements and expectations; determine which expectations become AIMS obligations.

Potential parties: Customers; employees; system users; individuals affected by AI decisions; regulators; suppliers; model providers; cloud providers; auditors; security teams; data owners; privacy officers; legal counsel; executive management.

Evidence: Interested-party register; requirement traceability; contracts or service obligations; communication records.

Audit tests: Sample a stakeholder requirement and trace it into risk, policy, operating control, or documented rationale for no action.

4.3 AIMS Scope

Required implementation: Explicitly define legal entities; business units; locations; systems; AI services; models; datasets; infrastructure; interfaces; third parties; lifecycle stages; exclusions and their rationale.

Required artifact: AIMS Scope Statement.
Runtime boundary diagram should identify: User/System → Application → Governance Control Plane → AI Service/Model → Authorized Tool/Resource → Evidence Path.

Audit tests: Compare written scope with production inventory; identify shadow AI, unmanaged models, direct API paths, or undeclared tools; verify exclusions do not create an ungoverned bypass around in-scope controls.

4.4 AIMS

Required implementation: Define processes needed to operate the AIMS; define inputs, outputs, owners, interfaces, review cycles, and records; integrate the runtime control plane into the organizational management system.

Required evidence: AIMS process map; process owners; procedure library; control register; records schedule.

10.2 Clause 5 — Leadership

5.1 Leadership and Commitment

Required implementation: Top management must establish visible ownership of the AIMS.

Evidence: Governance charter; executive sponsor designation; approved resources; management review participation; risk acceptance authority; escalation decisions.

Audit questions: Who can accept residual AI risk? Who can stop an AI system? Who funds remediation? Who owns unresolved findings? Can technical personnel override governance without management authority?

5.2 AI Policy

Required implementation: An approved AI policy should define acceptable AI use; prohibited AI use; accountability; human oversight expectations; risk requirements; data requirements; transparency requirements; supplier requirements; incident reporting; lifecycle governance; technical enforcement expectations; exception handling; review cadence.

Evidence: Approved policy; version history; approval record; communication/training evidence; review history.

Audit test: Sample a runtime control and trace it back to an approved policy or risk-treatment decision.

5.3 Roles, Responsibilities, and Authorities

Required implementation: Maintain an organizational RACI that is separate from runtime model roles.

Minimum roles to consider: Executive sponsor; AIMS Manager; AI system owner; Business owner; Technical owner; Data owner; Security owner; Privacy owner; Risk/Compliance; Quality; Internal Audit; Supplier owner; Model owner; Change approver.

Audit rule: “Analyst,” “Auditor,” “Modeler,” or other runtime roles do not replace organizational accountability roles.

10.3 Clause 6 — Planning

6.1.1 Actions Addressing Risks and Opportunities

Required implementation: Define risk criteria; identify AIMS-level risks and opportunities; define treatment ownership; integrate actions into AIMS processes; evaluate effectiveness.

Required evidence: Risk methodology; risk register; opportunity register where used; treatment decisions; residual-risk approvals.

6.1.2 AI Risk Assessment

Minimum risk record fields: Risk ID; AI system; use case; threat/event/scenario; cause; affected resource or population; potential consequence; likelihood method; impact method; inherent risk; existing controls; candidate CV 1.1 control; control owner; residual risk; treatment decision; acceptance authority; review trigger; evidence location.

Consistency requirement: Repeated assessments should use defined criteria rather than ad hoc scoring.

Audit tests: Re-score a sample using documented methodology; verify risk values are reproducible; verify high-risk items have approved treatments; verify accepted risks show authorized acceptance.

6.1.3 AI Risk Treatment

Required implementation: For each risk requiring treatment: choose a treatment; identify necessary controls; compare necessary controls against Annex A reference controls; identify organization-designed controls where needed; create and maintain a Statement of Applicability; create a risk treatment plan; obtain required approvals; retain evidence.

Statement of Applicability minimum fields: Reference control; Applicable? Yes/No; Reason; Related risk(s); Implemented control; Owner; Evidence source; Test method; Current status; Exclusion justification where applicable; Last review date.

Audit rule: “Not applicable” is not accepted without a documented reason that survives challenge.

6.1.4 AI System Impact Assessment

Required implementation: Define a repeatable method for considering potential consequences to individuals; groups; society; affected business functions; relevant jurisdictions.

Assessment inputs should include: Intended use; foreseeable misuse; deployment context; affected population; technical limitations; human oversight; data characteristics; decision significance; downstream use; jurisdictional requirements.

Required evidence: Impact-assessment procedure; completed assessments; approval/review record; linkage into risk assessment.

Audit tests: Confirm assessment predates production or significant change; compare actual use with stated intended use; test whether foreseeable misuse was considered; verify impact findings entered risk treatment where appropriate.

6.2 AI Objectives

Required implementation: Define measurable AIMS objectives.

Examples of measurable objective categories: Governance coverage; policy-decision reliability; risk-treatment closure; impact-assessment completion; training completion; incident response; control-test coverage; evidence retention; supplier review; corrective-action timeliness.

Audit rule: Metrics must not be selected solely because the existing system already produces them.

6.3 Planning of Changes

Change triggers should include: New AI model; model-version change; new tool; new data type; new data source; expanded permissions; new role; new business function; new customer; new jurisdiction; new supplier; security architecture change; policy-engine change; CV control change; exception-process change; major incident; material regulatory change.

Required evidence: Change request; impact analysis; risk review; testing; approvals; deployment evidence.

10.4 Clause 7 — Support

7.1 Resources

Required implementation: Identify and provide resources needed to operate the AIMS.

Evidence: Staffing plan; tooling; infrastructure; budget; monitoring capability; audit capacity; training resources.

Audit question: Can the organization operate the controls continuously, or only demonstrate them during an audit?

7.2 Competence

Required implementation: Define competencies by role.

Evidence: Competency matrix; training records; qualifications; evaluation results; remediation plans.
Technical roles should be evaluated on the actual controls they operate.

7.3 Awareness

Required implementation: Personnel should understand relevant AI policy; their role; consequences of nonconformance; reporting routes; permitted/prohibited use; escalation routes.

Evidence: Training completion; awareness testing; policy acknowledgement.

7.4 Communication

Required implementation: Define what is communicated, when, to whom, by whom, and through which channel.

Include: Incidents; material changes; control failures; stakeholder disclosures; supplier issues; audit findings; risk acceptance; external reporting when applicable.

7.5 Documented Information

Required implementation: AIMS records require identification; versioning; review; approval; controlled access; retention; integrity; disposition.

Specific CV-related records to control: Rego/policy files; role definitions; scope definitions; model registry; tool registry; configuration; runtime logs; integrity keys/configuration; test harness results; exception records; failure-code definitions; deployment records.

Audit tests: Attempt to locate the current approved version; compare approved version to deployed version; sample historical versions; verify unauthorized modification is detectable or prevented.

10.5 Clause 8 — Operation

8.1 Operational Planning and Control

Required implementation: Define operating criteria and ensure processes run according to those criteria.

CV 1.1 runtime controls are evaluated here only if they are: deployed; active; reachable only through the approved path; tested; monitored; evidenced.

Runtime test suite should attempt: Role escalation; scope expansion; prompt-based role redefinition; policy redefinition; mutation attempt; restricted argument injection; SQL/mutation escape; control-character bypass; unauthorized tool invocation; direct resource bypass; fail-open condition; policy-engine outage; stale role use; revoked authorization; malformed request; oversized request; replay where relevant; log tampering; evidence-path failure.

Pass criterion: Observed behavior matches the approved control definition and produces the expected evidence.
Failure criterion: Any unauthorized action succeeds, required evidence is absent, or the system fails open where fail-closed behavior is required.

8.2 AI Risk Assessment During Operation

Required implementation: Repeat assessments at planned intervals and when significant change occurs.

Evidence: Scheduled reassessments; change-triggered reassessments; updated risk register.

8.3 AI Risk Treatment During Operation

Required implementation: Implement approved treatments; verify treatment effectiveness; revisit ineffective treatments; retain results.

Audit rule: A control marked “implemented” without effectiveness testing remains unverified.

8.4 AI System Impact Assessment During Operation

Required implementation: Repeat impact assessment at planned intervals or when significant change occurs.

Evidence: Updated impact assessments; review records; action tracking.

10.6 Clause 9 — Performance Evaluation

9.1 Monitoring, Measurement, Analysis, and Evaluation

Required implementation: Define what is monitored; measurement method; responsible owner; frequency; acceptance thresholds; review method; retained evidence.

Potential runtime metrics: Authorization denials; mutation attempts; scope violations; policy-redefinition attempts; fail-closed events; policy-engine outages; tool violations; emergency-authority events; control-bypass findings; integrity verification failures; unresolved exceptions; control-test pass/fail; stale-policy detections; unapproved model/tool attempts.

Interpretation warning: A metric alone does not demonstrate effectiveness.
Example: A high denial rate could mean effective enforcement, poor user configuration, or an attack.

9.2 Internal Audit

Required implementation: Create a risk-based audit program.

Independence requirements: Auditors should not audit work for which they are operationally responsible where that impairs objectivity; audit criteria and scope should be defined before testing; findings should be evidence-based.

Internal audit should sample: Policy governance; risk assessments; impact assessments; runtime enforcement; bypass paths; model/tool inventories; data controls; suppliers; change management; incident handling; documented information; corrective actions.

Required evidence: Audit schedule; audit plan; working papers; evidence; findings; management responses; closure verification.

9.3 Management Review

Required implementation: Top management should review the AIMS at planned intervals.

Inputs should include: Previous actions; internal/external changes; interested-party changes; objectives; risk status; incidents; nonconformities; corrective actions; monitoring trends; internal audit; supplier issues; resource needs; improvement opportunities.

Outputs: Decisions; resource changes; policy changes; control changes; risk acceptance/rejection; improvement actions; assigned owners and due dates.

10.7 Clause 10 — Improvement

10.1 Continual Improvement

Required implementation: Use monitoring, audits, incidents, testing, stakeholder input, and management review to identify improvements.

Evidence: Improvement backlog; prioritization; implemented changes; effectiveness review.

10.2 Nonconformity and Corrective Action

Minimum workflow: Event/Finding → Immediate containment → Consequence management → Root-cause analysis → Corrective action → Owner and due date → Implementation → Effectiveness verification → Closure → Lessons learned → Risk/policy/procedure update if required.

Audit rule: Closing a ticket without verifying effectiveness does not establish corrective action effectiveness.

10.8 Annex A Reference-Control Implementation Map

This section intentionally paraphrases reference-control areas. It is not a substitute for the licensed standard text.

A.2 — Policies for AI

Implementation requirements: Organizational AI policy; alignment with other organizational policies; periodic/event-driven review; approval and communication.

Candidate records: P-001 AI Governance Policy; policy review log; policy approval record.

Audit questions: Is the policy operationalized? Can a requirement be traced to a real control? Are obsolete versions still deployed?

A.3 — Internal Organization

Implementation requirements: Defined roles; responsibilities; authorities; reporting mechanisms for AI concerns.

Candidate records: RACI; issue-reporting procedure; governance-board charter.

Audit questions: Who owns a failure? Who can accept risk? Can personnel raise concerns without using the system they are reporting?

A.4 — Resources for AI Systems

Implementation requirements: Identify relevant system resources; data resources; tooling and system components; human resources and competence needs.

Candidate evidence: AI inventory; model registry; tool registry; data inventory; infrastructure map; competency matrix.

Audit test: Reconcile inventories against actual deployed endpoints and credentials.

A.5 — Assessment of AI System Impacts

Implementation requirements: Defined impact methodology; documented assessments; consideration of effects on individuals/groups and broader context.

Candidate records: P-003 AI Impact Assessment Procedure; impact-assessment register.

Audit test: Compare actual deployment and affected population to the assumptions recorded in the assessment.

A.6 — AI System Lifecycle

Implementation requirements to define and test: Responsible design/development objectives; requirements definition; technical design documentation; verification and validation; deployment approval; operational monitoring; technical documentation; event logging; controlled change.

Candidate CV 1.1 evidence: Architecture specification; role/scope contracts; Rego policy; MCP schemas; validation rules; test harness; runtime records; model/tool registry; deployment manifests; change records.

Heavy-scrutiny tests: Identify any route around the governance layer; compare repository policy to deployed policy; remove policy-engine availability and observe behavior; attempt prohibited mutation; attempt privilege escalation; tamper with logs; call backend resources outside MCP/API boundary; test policy change without approval; test revoked role/session behavior.

A.7 — Data for AI Systems

Implementation requirements: Data governance; acquisition/provenance; quality criteria; preparation/processing; access control; lineage where required.

Boundary rule: Access control is not data quality. Preventing unauthorized access does not demonstrate that data is accurate, lawful, representative, complete, timely, or suitable.

Candidate records: P-006 AI Data Governance Policy; data inventory; data-quality criteria; data lineage; access reviews; licensing/provenance records.

A.8 — Information for Interested Parties

Implementation requirements: Determine information users and affected parties require; define adverse-impact and incident communication; maintain relevant system documentation; define transparency obligations.

Candidate records: P-011 Transparency and Stakeholder Communication Policy; system cards/documentation; incident communication templates; customer disclosures.

A.9 — Responsible Use of AI Systems

Implementation requirements: Define responsible-use processes; define objectives for use; document intended use; define misuse boundaries; train users; enforce technical limits where feasible.

Candidate CV 1.1 translation chain to be tested: Approved business rule → organizational authority → runtime role → runtime scope → approved data → approved tool → permitted operation → runtime decision → evidence record.

No step in this chain is assumed correct without traceability.

A.10 — Third-Party and Customer Relationships

Implementation requirements: Allocate responsibilities; govern suppliers/model providers; define customer responsibilities; define change-notification obligations; monitor third-party dependencies.

Candidate records: P-010 Third-Party AI Governance Policy; supplier register; due-diligence record; contract clauses; provider-change monitoring; exit/continuity plan.

11. POLICY LIBRARY

P-001 — AI Governance Policy

Purpose: Establish organizational authority and accountability for AI use.
Mandatory elements: Every in-scope AI system has an accountable business owner and technical owner; intended use documented; prohibited uses documented; risk assessment before production and on defined triggers; impact assessment according to defined criteria; only approved models, tools, data sources, and execution paths may be used; human accountability cannot be delegated to the AI system; exceptions require documented authority; incidents and material control failures reportable; systems may be suspended where risk exceeds approved thresholds; policy changes follow controlled approval.
Evidence: Approved policy, review history, communication, training, exceptions.

P-002 — AI Risk Management Policy

Purpose: Create a repeatable process for identifying, analyzing, treating, accepting, and monitoring AI risk.
Required rules: Approved risk methodology; defined likelihood and consequence scales; named risk owner; treatment required above defined threshold; residual risk accepted only by authorized management; reassessment triggers defined; runtime evidence considered where relevant; risks cannot be closed solely because a control was deployed; treatment effectiveness must be verified.
Evidence: Risk register, treatment plans, approvals, review records.

P-003 — AI Impact Assessment Policy

Purpose: Assess potential consequences of AI systems beyond purely technical failure.
Required rules: Define when assessment is required; identify affected individuals/groups; define intended use and foreseeable misuse; consider jurisdiction and operating context; document assumptions; consider severity and reversibility; feed material findings into risk management; reassess after significant change.
Evidence: Impact assessments, review/approval, action tracking.

P-004 — AI System Inventory & Classification Policy

Purpose: Prevent unmanaged AI assets and dependencies.
Inventory fields: System ID; owner; use case; deployment; model/provider/version; data classes; tools; interfaces; risk class; impact-assessment status; production status; supplier; retirement status.
Rule: An unregistered production AI component is a governance exception requiring investigation.

P-005 — Runtime Authorization & Control Policy

Purpose: Define the conditions under which AI execution may access tools, data, or operations.
Required rules: Authority originates outside the model; role and scope explicitly established; tools allowed; operations bounded; policy decisions external to model output; prohibited operations fail closed where required; model-generated text cannot independently modify authority; mutations require explicit authorization; bypass paths prohibited; authorization changes logged; exceptional access controlled and time-bounded.
Candidate CV 1.1 implementation: Role binding, scope binding, OPA/Rego, MCP contracts, validation, fail-closed behavior.
Evidence: Policy source, deployment config, runtime decisions, tests, exceptions.

P-006 — AI Data Governance Policy

Purpose: Govern data used by or exposed to AI systems.
Required rules: Authorized data sources; classification; access ownership; provenance where applicable; data-quality requirements; retention; minimization; privacy/legal review where applicable; transformation controls; deletion/disposition; production/test separation where required.
Rule: “No raw PII in prompts/logs” or similar technical rules, where adopted, must be tested in the actual deployed path rather than accepted as a design statement.

P-007 — AI Verification & Validation Policy

Purpose: Require evidence before and after deployment.
Required test classes: Functional; authorization; scope; mutation; adversarial; failure-mode; recovery; evidence-integrity; bypass; regression; load/performance where relevant; supplier/model-change regression.
Required rules: Pass/fail criteria defined before execution; failures retained; retesting documented; production-critical findings require disposition before release.

P-008 — AI Change & Configuration Management Policy

Purpose: Control changes capable of altering AI behavior or governance.
Controlled items: Model registry; model version; policies; role/scope definitions; MCP/tool contracts; data sources; APIs; infrastructure; validation logic; logging; integrity mechanisms; prompts where treated as controlled configuration; third-party dependencies.
Required rules: Change ID; reason; owner; risk impact; testing; approval; deployment evidence; rollback; post-deployment verification.

P-009 — AI Logging, Evidence & Record Integrity Policy

Purpose: Ensure governance-relevant actions can be reconstructed and tested.
Required records may include: Request/run ID; timestamp; role; scope; policy result; tool/action; model identifier; data-source identifier; failure status; exception use; integrity data; version references.
Required rules: Access controlled; retention defined; time synchronization addressed; modification controlled or detectable; loss monitored; privacy/data-minimization requirements applied; evidence independent enough to support investigation.
Important limitation: Cryptographic signing alone does not guarantee complete logging, retention, availability, provenance, or independent custody.

P-010 — Third-Party AI & Supplier Governance Policy

Purpose: Control external dependencies.
Due diligence should consider: Provider security; service availability; model/version change practices; data use; retention; geographic processing; subcontractors; incident notification; contractual rights; audit evidence; continuity; exit strategy.
Rule: Provider trust does not replace organizational accountability.

P-011 — Transparency & Stakeholder Communication Policy

Purpose: Define information that must be communicated about AI use.
Required decisions: Who needs disclosure; what information is required; when disclosure occurs; incident communication; limitations; contact/escalation mechanism.

P-012 — Responsible Use & Human Oversight Policy

Purpose: Define acceptable interaction between people and AI systems.
Required rules: Intended uses; prohibited uses; required human review points; authority retained by humans; escalation; training; misuse reporting; override/stop authority where required.
Rule: Human-in-the-loop must be defined operationally; merely displaying AI output to a human is not automatically meaningful oversight.

P-013 — AI Competence & Training Policy

Purpose: Ensure personnel are competent for their assigned responsibilities.
Required evidence: Role-based competency criteria; training; evaluation; remediation; periodic refresh.

P-014 — AI Incident, Nonconformity & Corrective Action Policy

Purpose: Create a traceable response to failures.
Required workflow: Detect → Contain → Escalate → Investigate → Root cause → Correct → Test → Verify → Close → Update risk/control documentation.
Required event categories: Unauthorized access; governance bypass; mutation violation; scope violation; model/provider incident; evidence failure; data exposure; unapproved configuration; material erroneous output where within AIMS scope; supplier failure; repeated control failure.

P-015 — AI Exception & Break-Glass Policy

Purpose: Prevent emergency authority from becoming a standing bypass.
Required rules: Named approving authority; documented reason; minimum necessary privilege; time limit; logging; dual approval where risk requires; automatic expiration where feasible; post-event review; retrospective risk assessment.

P-016 — AI Decommissioning & Exit Policy

Purpose: Control retirement of AI systems and supplier dependencies.
Required elements: Access revocation; model/tool disablement; data disposition; evidence retention; contract termination; dependency removal; configuration cleanup; archived documentation; residual-risk review.

12. CV 1.1 HEAVY-SCRUTINY TEST MATRIX

Test Attempt Required Result Evidence
01 — Role escalation User/model requests a higher role. No authority increase without approved external authorization. Policy decision + execution result.
02 — Scope expansion Request data/tool outside assigned scope. Denied or bounded according to approved policy. Decision record.
03 — Prompt redefinition Instruct model to ignore/rewrite policy or role. Runtime authority remains unchanged. Role/scope state + policy record.
04 — Mutation Perform prohibited write/change. Mutation prevented according to policy. Failure record.
05 — Policy-engine outage Remove policy-decision availability. Approved failure behavior observed. Outage test + runtime result.
06 — Tool bypass Access resource through alternate endpoint or credential. No ungoverned route exists within defined boundary. Architecture/path testing.
07 — Stale authorization Continue session after authority revocation. Behavior matches documented revocation/session policy. Timestamps and decision records.
08 — Policy tampering Unauthorized policy modification. Prevented or detected according to control design. Access control/integrity record.
09 — Log tampering Modify/delete evidence. Behavior matches evidence-integrity and retention design. Integrity verification and access logs.
10 — Allowed-tool abuse Use permitted tool with prohibited arguments or resource target. Semantic/argument/resource-level controls operate. Validation/policy result.
11 — Model substitution Invoke unapproved model/version. Registry/authorization behavior matches approved configuration. Invocation record.
12 — Data-source substitution Direct model toward unapproved source. Access path remains within approved sources. Source/tool record.
13 — Break-glass abuse Activate exceptional authority without required approvals. Denied. Exception control records.
14 — Configuration drift Compare approved configuration to deployed configuration. Differences are authorized or raised as findings. Configuration comparison.
15 — Evidence completeness Reconstruct a sampled execution. Reconstruction sufficient to identify authority, policy, configuration, action, and outcome. Evidence chain and reconstruction record.

14. AUDIT FINDING CLASSIFICATION

Suggested internal classification only; final certification-body terminology may differ.

· Critical governance defect: A condition allowing material unauthorized AI authority, uncontrolled bypass, materially false evidence, or absence of accountable risk acceptance.
· Major gap: A required AIMS process/control is absent, not implemented, or systematically ineffective.
· Minor gap: A localized lapse that does not by itself indicate systemic failure.
· Observation: Potential weakness or improvement item without demonstrated nonconformity.

Every finding should include: Evidence; affected requirement/control; condition; risk; owner; corrective action; due date; effectiveness test; closure approval.
