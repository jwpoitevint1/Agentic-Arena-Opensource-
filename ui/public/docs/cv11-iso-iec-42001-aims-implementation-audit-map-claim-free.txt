CV 1.1 — ISO/IEC 42001 AIMS IMPLEMENTATION & AUDIT MAP
CLAIM-FREE / EVIDENCE-BASED WORKING DOCUMENT
Document status: Working implementation framework
Standard basis: ISO/IEC 42001:2023
Purpose: Define what an organization would need to implement, document, test, operate, and
evidence if CV 1.1 is used as a runtime AI-governance control layer inside an Artificial
Intelligence Management System (AIMS).
IMPORTANT BOUNDARY
This document makes no claim that CV 1.1 conforms to, satisfies, certifies, or guarantees any
ISO/IEC 42001 requirement.
Every mapping below is a candidate implementation relationship only.
A requirement is not considered met until the organization has:
1. defined the control;
2. assigned accountable ownership;
3. implemented it in the actual environment;
4. generated objective evidence;
5. tested the implementation;
6. retained the evidence;
7. completed internal review; and
8. survived independent audit.
Blank, missing, stale, untested, or contradictory evidence is treated as a gap.
======================================================================
1. GOVERNANCE MODEL
======================================================================
The organization should define four distinct control layers.
Layer 1 — Governance
Purpose: Establish authority, policy, objectives, risk appetite, and accountability.
Potential owners: Executive leadership, AI Governance Board, AIMS Manager.
Required evidence: Approved policy, charter, objectives, risk criteria, meeting records,
approvals.
Layer 2 — Management
Purpose: Convert governance requirements into risk treatment, lifecycle, impact, supplier, data,
and operating processes.
Potential owners: Risk, Compliance, Privacy, Security, Quality, Product/System owners.
Required evidence: Risk register, impact assessments, treatment plans, Statement of
Applicability, procedures, supplier records.
Layer 3 — Runtime Enforcement
Purpose: Enforce approved technical restrictions during AI execution.
Candidate CV 1.1 mechanisms: role binding, scope binding, OPA/Rego policy decisions,
MCP/tool boundaries, validation contracts, fail-closed handling, mutation restrictions, audit
evidence, integrity controls.
Required evidence: Configuration, policy source, deployment records, policy decisions,
execution records, failure records, test outputs.
Layer 4 — Assurance
Purpose: Determine whether the management system and technical controls actually operate
as intended.
Potential owners: Internal Audit, independent security review, external conformity assessment.
Required evidence: Audit program, audit results, nonconformities, corrective actions,
management reviews.
Segregation rule:
No technical implementation team should be treated as the sole independent assessor of its
own implementation.

2. CV 1.1 TECHNICAL BOUNDARY TO BE TESTED
======================================================================
The following are candidate runtime mechanisms to be independently verified in each
deployment:
- external role binding;
- external scope binding;
- policy decision outside the model;
- OPA/Rego or equivalent policy enforcement;
- MCP/API capability boundaries;
- identifier validation;
- argument validation;
- text/control validation;
- redefinition-language controls;
- mutation restrictions;
- read/write separation where required;
- fail-closed behavior;
- circuit-breaker or isolation behavior;
- immutable or controlled role assignment;
- integrity-protected runtime records;
- auditable execution evidence;
- controlled exceptional authority;
- bounded failure modes;
- approved tool allowlists;
- approved model allowlists;
- approved data-source boundaries.
AUDIT RULE:
The presence of source code is not evidence that the control is operating in production.
The auditor/tester must verify the deployed path, not merely the repository.

3. CLAUSE 4 — CONTEXT OF THE ORGANIZATION
======================================================================
4.1 Organizational context
Required implementation:
- Identify internal and external issues affecting the AIMS.
- Identify business, technical, legal, regulatory, security, privacy, quality, ethical, operational, and
AI-specific conditions.
- Identify dependencies that could alter runtime governance effectiveness.
Minimum evidence:
- Context register.
- Applicable-law and obligation register.
- Architecture dependency map.
- Business-use-case inventory.
- Environmental assumptions.
- Review date and owner.
Audit tests:
- Compare the context register with actual deployments.
- Check whether new models, suppliers, jurisdictions, or business uses were captured.
- Sample changes and verify context updates occurred.
- Challenge assumptions documented as “out of scope.”
Failure condition:
Material operating conditions exist that are absent from the context analysis.
4.2 Interested parties
Required implementation:
- Identify relevant internal and external interested parties.
- Define their relevant requirements and expectations.
- Determine which expectations become AIMS obligations.
Potential parties:
- customers;
- employees;
- system users;
- individuals affected by AI decisions;
- regulators;
- suppliers;
- model providers;
- cloud providers;
- auditors;
- security teams;
- data owners;
- privacy officers;
- legal counsel;
- executive management.
Evidence:
- Interested-party register.
- Requirement traceability.
- Contracts or service obligations.
- Communication records.
Audit tests:
- Sample a stakeholder requirement and trace it into risk, policy, operating control, or
documented rationale for no action.
4.3 AIMS scope
Required implementation:
The organization must explicitly define:
- legal entities;
- business units;
- locations;
- systems;
- AI services;
- models;
- datasets;
- infrastructure;
- interfaces;
- third parties;
- lifecycle stages;
- exclusions and their rationale.
Required artifact:
AIMS Scope Statement.
Runtime boundary diagram should identify, at minimum:
User/System -> Application -> Governance Control Plane -> AI Service/Model -> Authorized
Tool/Resource -> Evidence Path.
Audit tests:
- Compare the written scope with production inventory.
- Identify shadow AI, unmanaged models, direct API paths, or undeclared tools.
- Verify exclusions do not create an ungoverned bypass around in-scope controls.
4.4 AIMS
Required implementation:
- Define the processes needed to operate the AIMS.
- Define inputs, outputs, owners, interfaces, review cycles, and records.
- Integrate the runtime control plane into the organizational management system.
Required evidence:
- AIMS process map.
- Process owners.
- Procedure library.
- Control register.
- Records schedule.

Evidence:

- Executive sponsor designation.
- Approved resources.
- Management review participation.
- Risk acceptance authority.
- Escalation decisions.
Audit questions:
- Who can accept residual AI risk?
- Who can stop an AI system?
- Who funds remediation?
- Who owns unresolved findings?
- Can technical personnel override governance without management authority?
5.2 AI policy
Required implementation:
An approved AI policy should define:
- acceptable AI use;
- prohibited AI use;
- accountability;
- human oversight expectations;
- risk requirements;
- data requirements;
- transparency requirements;
- supplier requirements;
- incident reporting;
- lifecycle governance;
- technical enforcement expectations;
- exception handling;
- review cadence.
Evidence:
- Approved policy.
- Version history.
- approval record;
- communication/training evidence;
- review history.
Audit test:
Sample a runtime control and trace it back to an approved policy or risk-treatment decision.
5.3 Roles, responsibilities, and authorities
Required implementation:
Maintain an organizational RACI that is separate from runtime model roles.
Minimum roles to consider:
- Executive sponsor;
- AIMS Manager;
- AI system owner;
- Business owner;
- Technical owner;
- Data owner;
- Security owner;
- Privacy owner;
- Risk/Compliance;
- Quality;

- Model owner;
- Change approver.
Audit rule:
“Analyst,” “Auditor,” “Modeler,” or other runtime roles do not replace organizational
accountability roles.

5. CLAUSE 6 — PLANNING
======================================================================
6.1.1 Actions addressing risks and opportunities
Required implementation:
- Define risk criteria.
- Identify AIMS-level risks and opportunities.
- Define treatment ownership.
- Integrate actions into AIMS processes.
- Evaluate effectiveness.
Required evidence:
- Risk methodology.
- Risk register.
- opportunity register where used;
- treatment decisions;
- residual-risk approvals.
6.1.2 AI risk assessment
Minimum risk record fields:
- Risk ID.
- AI system.
- use case;
- threat/event/scenario;
- cause;
- affected resource or population;
- potential consequence;
- likelihood method;
- impact method;
- inherent risk;
- existing controls;
- candidate CV 1.1 control;
- control owner;
- residual risk;
- treatment decision;
- acceptance authority;
- review trigger;
- evidence location.
Consistency requirement:
Repeated assessments should use defined criteria rather than ad hoc scoring.
Audit tests:
- Re-score a sample using the documented methodology.
- Verify risk values are reproducible.
- Verify high-risk items have approved treatments.
- Verify accepted risks show authorized acceptance.
6.1.3 AI risk treatment
Required implementation:
For each risk requiring treatment:
- choose a treatment;
- identify necessary controls;
- compare necessary controls against Annex A reference controls;
- identify organization-designed controls where needed;
- create and maintain a Statement of Applicability;
- create a risk treatment plan;
- obtain required approvals;
- retain evidence.
Statement of Applicability minimum fields:
- Reference control.
- Applicable? Yes/No.
- Reason.
- Related risk(s).
- Implemented control.
- Owner.
- Evidence source.
- Test method.
- Current status.
- Exclusion justification where applicable.
- Last review date.
Audit rule:
“Not applicable” is not accepted without a documented reason that survives challenge.
6.1.4 AI system impact assessment
Required implementation:
Define a repeatable method for considering potential consequences to:
- individuals;
- groups;
- society;
- affected business functions;
- relevant jurisdictions.
Assessment inputs should include:
- intended use;
- foreseeable misuse;
- deployment context;
- affected population;
- technical limitations;
- human oversight;
- data characteristics;
- decision significance;
- downstream use;
- jurisdictional requirements.
Required evidence:
- Impact-assessment procedure.
- Completed assessments.
- Approval/review record.
- Linkage into risk assessment.
Audit tests:
- Confirm assessment predates production or significant change.
- Compare actual use with stated intended use.
- Test whether foreseeable misuse was considered.
- Verify impact findings entered risk treatment where appropriate.
6.2 AI objectives
Required implementation:
Define measurable AIMS objectives.
Examples of measurable objective categories:
- governance coverage;
- policy-decision reliability;
- risk-treatment closure;
- impact-assessment completion;
- training completion;
- incident response;
- control-test coverage;
- evidence retention;
- supplier review;
- corrective-action timeliness.
Audit rule:
Metrics must not be selected solely because the existing system already produces them.
6.3 Planning of changes
Change triggers should include:
- new AI model;
- model-version change;
- new tool;
- new data type;
- new data source;
- expanded permissions;
- new role;
- new business function;
- new customer;
- new jurisdiction;
- new supplier;
- security architecture change;
- policy-engine change;
- CV control change;
- exception-process change;
- major incident;

Required evidence:

- impact analysis;
- risk review;
- testing;
- approvals;

- rollback plan;
- post-change verification.

7. CLAUSE 8 — OPERATION
======================================================================
8.1 Operational planning and control
Required implementation:
Define operating criteria and ensure processes run according to those criteria.
CV 1.1 runtime controls are evaluated here only if they are:
- deployed;
- active;
- reachable only through the approved path;
- tested;
- monitored;
- evidenced.
Runtime test suite should attempt:
- role escalation;
- scope expansion;
- prompt-based role redefinition;
- policy redefinition;
- mutation attempt;
- restricted argument injection;
- SQL/mutation escape;
- control-character bypass;
- unauthorized tool invocation;
- direct resource bypass;
- fail-open condition;
- policy-engine outage;
- stale role use;
- revoked authorization;
- malformed request;
- oversized request;
- replay where relevant;
- log tampering;
- evidence-path failure.
Pass criterion:
Observed behavior matches the approved control definition and produces the expected
evidence.
Failure criterion:
Any unauthorized action succeeds, required evidence is absent, or the system fails open where
fail-closed behavior is required.
8.2 AI risk assessment during operation
Required implementation:
Repeat assessments at planned intervals and when significant change occurs.
Evidence:
- scheduled reassessments;
- change-triggered reassessments;
- updated risk register.
8.3 AI risk treatment during operation
Required implementation:
- implement approved treatments;
- verify treatment effectiveness;
- revisit ineffective treatments;
- retain results.
Audit rule:
A control marked “implemented” without effectiveness testing remains unverified.
8.4 AI system impact assessment during operation
Required implementation:
Repeat impact assessment at planned intervals or when significant change occurs.
Evidence:
- updated assessment;
- change trigger;
- review approval;
- downstream risk updates.

8. CLAUSE 9 — PERFORMANCE EVALUATION
======================================================================
9.1 Monitoring, measurement, analysis, and evaluation
Required implementation:
Define:
- what is monitored;
- measurement method;
- responsible owner;
- frequency;
- acceptance thresholds;
- review method;
- retained evidence.
Potential runtime metrics:
- authorization denials;
- mutation attempts;
- scope violations;
- policy-redefinition attempts;
- fail-closed events;
- policy-engine outages;
- tool violations;
- emergency-authority events;
- control-bypass findings;
- integrity verification failures;
- unresolved exceptions;
- control-test pass/fail;
- stale-policy detections;
- unapproved model/tool attempts.
Interpretation warning:
A metric alone does not demonstrate effectiveness.
Example: a high denial rate could mean effective enforcement, poor user configuration, or an
attack.
9.2 Internal audit
Required implementation:
Create a risk-based audit program.
Independence requirements:
- auditors should not audit work for which they are operationally responsible where that impairs
objectivity;
- audit criteria and scope should be defined before testing;
- findings should be evidence-based.
Internal audit should sample:
- policy governance;
- risk assessments;
- impact assessments;
- runtime enforcement;
- bypass paths;
- model/tool inventories;
- data controls;
- suppliers;
- change management;
- incident handling;
- documented information;
- corrective actions.
Required evidence:
- audit schedule;
- audit plan;
- working papers;
- evidence;
- findings;
- management responses;
- closure verification.
9.3 Management review
Required implementation:
Top management should review the AIMS at planned intervals.
Inputs should include:
- previous actions;
- internal/external changes;
- interested-party changes;
- objectives;
- risk status;
- incidents;
- nonconformities;
- corrective actions;
- monitoring trends;
- internal audit;
- supplier issues;
- resource needs;
- improvement opportunities.
Outputs:
- decisions;
- resource changes;
- policy changes;
- control changes;
- risk acceptance/rejection;
- improvement actions;
- assigned owners and due dates.

9. CLAUSE 10 — IMPROVEMENT
======================================================================
10.1 Continual improvement
Required implementation:
Use monitoring, audits, incidents, testing, stakeholder input, and management review to identify
improvements.
Evidence:
- improvement backlog;
- prioritization;
- implemented changes;
- effectiveness review.
10.2 Nonconformity and corrective action
Minimum workflow:
Event/Finding
-> Immediate containment
-> Consequence management
-> Root-cause analysis
-> Corrective action
-> Owner and due date
-> Implementation
-> Effectiveness verification
-> Closure
-> Lessons learned
-> Risk/policy/procedure update if required
Audit rule:
Closing a ticket without verifying effectiveness does not establish corrective action
effectiveness.

11. POLICY LIBRARY
======================================================================
P-001 — AI GOVERNANCE POLICY
Purpose:
Establish organizational authority and accountability for AI use.
Mandatory policy elements:
- every in-scope AI system has an accountable business owner;
- every in-scope AI system has a technical owner;
- intended use is documented;
- prohibited uses are documented;
- risk assessment occurs before production and on defined triggers;
- impact assessment occurs according to defined criteria;
- only approved models, tools, data sources, and execution paths may be used;
- human accountability cannot be delegated to the AI system;
- exceptions require documented authority;
- incidents and material control failures are reportable;
- systems may be suspended where risk exceeds approved thresholds;
- policy changes follow controlled approval.
Evidence:
Approved policy, review history, communication, training, exceptions.
P-002 — AI RISK MANAGEMENT POLICY
Purpose:
Create a repeatable process for identifying, analyzing, treating, accepting, and monitoring AI
risk.
Required rules:
- approved risk methodology;
- defined likelihood and consequence scales;
- named risk owner;
- treatment required above defined threshold;
- residual risk accepted only by authorized management;
- reassessment triggers defined;
- runtime evidence considered where relevant;
- risks cannot be closed solely because a control was deployed;
- treatment effectiveness must be verified.
Evidence:
Risk register, treatment plans, approvals, review records.
P-003 — AI IMPACT ASSESSMENT POLICY
Purpose:
Assess potential consequences of AI systems beyond purely technical failure.
Required rules:
- define when assessment is required;
- identify affected individuals/groups;
- define intended use and foreseeable misuse;
- consider jurisdiction and operating context;
- document assumptions;
- consider severity and reversibility;
- feed material findings into risk management;
- reassess after significant change.
Evidence:
Impact assessments, review/approval, action tracking.
P-004 — AI SYSTEM INVENTORY & CLASSIFICATION POLICY
Purpose:
Prevent unmanaged AI assets and dependencies.
Inventory fields:
- system ID;
- owner;
- use case;
- deployment;
- model/provider/version;
- data classes;
- tools;
- interfaces;
- risk class;
- impact-assessment status;
- production status;
- supplier;
- retirement status.
Rule:
An unregistered production AI component is a governance exception requiring investigation.
P-005 — RUNTIME AUTHORIZATION & CONTROL POLICY
Purpose:
Define the conditions under which AI execution may access tools, data, or operations.
Required rules:
- authority originates outside the model;
- role and scope are explicitly established;
- tools are allowlisted;
- operations are bounded;
- policy decisions are external to model output;
- prohibited operations fail closed where required;
- model-generated text cannot independently modify authority;
- mutations require explicit authorization;
- bypass paths are prohibited;
- authorization changes are logged;
- exceptional access is controlled and time-bounded.
Candidate CV 1.1 implementation:
Role binding, scope binding, OPA/Rego, MCP contracts, validation, fail-closed behavior.
Evidence:
Policy source, deployment config, runtime decisions, tests, exceptions.
P-006 — AI DATA GOVERNANCE POLICY
Purpose:
Govern data used by or exposed to AI systems.
Required rules:
- authorized data sources;
- classification;
- access ownership;
- provenance where applicable;
- data-quality requirements;
- retention;
- minimization;
- privacy/legal review where applicable;
- transformation controls;
- deletion/disposition;
- production/test separation where required.
Rule:
“No raw PII in prompts/logs” or similar technical rules, where adopted, must be tested in the
actual deployed path rather than accepted as a design statement.
P-007 — AI VERIFICATION & VALIDATION POLICY
Purpose:
Require evidence before and after deployment.
Required test classes:
- functional;
- authorization;
- scope;
- mutation;
- adversarial;
- failure-mode;
- recovery;
- evidence-integrity;
- bypass;
- regression;
- load/performance where relevant;
- supplier/model-change regression.
Required rules:
- pass/fail criteria defined before execution;
- failures retained;
- retesting documented;
- production-critical findings require disposition before release.
P-008 — AI CHANGE & CONFIGURATION MANAGEMENT POLICY
Purpose:
Control changes capable of altering AI behavior or governance.
Controlled items:
- model registry;
- model version;
- policies;
- role/scope definitions;
- MCP/tool contracts;
- data sources;
- APIs;
- infrastructure;
- validation logic;
- logging;
- integrity mechanisms;
- prompts where treated as controlled configuration;
- third-party dependencies.
Required rules:
- change ID;
- reason;
- owner;
- risk impact;
- testing;
- approval;
- deployment evidence;
- rollback;
- post-deployment verification.
P-009 — AI LOGGING, EVIDENCE & RECORD INTEGRITY POLICY
Purpose:
Ensure governance-relevant actions can be reconstructed and tested.
Required records should be defined by the organization and may include:
- request/run ID;
- timestamp;
- role;
- scope;
- policy result;
- tool/action;
- model identifier;
- data-source identifier;
- failure status;
- exception use;
- integrity data;
- version references.
Required rules:
- access controlled;
- retention defined;
- time synchronization addressed;
- modification controlled or detectable;
- loss monitored;
- privacy/data-minimization requirements applied;
- evidence independent enough to support investigation.
Important limitation:
Cryptographic signing alone does not guarantee complete logging, retention, availability,
provenance, or independent custody.
P-010 — THIRD-PARTY AI & SUPPLIER GOVERNANCE POLICY
Purpose:
Control external dependencies.
Due diligence should consider:

- service availability;
- model/version change practices;
- data use;
- retention;
- geographic processing;
- subcontractors;
- incident notification;
- contractual rights;
- audit evidence;
- continuity;

Provider trust does not replace organizational accountability.
P-011 — TRANSPARENCY & STAKEHOLDER COMMUNICATION POLICY
Purpose:
Define information that must be communicated about AI use.
Required decisions:
- who needs disclosure;
- what information is required;
- when disclosure occurs;
- incident communication;
- limitations;
- contact/escalation mechanism.
P-012 — RESPONSIBLE USE & HUMAN OVERSIGHT POLICY
Purpose:
Define acceptable interaction between people and AI systems.
Required rules:
- intended uses;
- prohibited uses;
- required human review points;
- authority retained by humans;
- escalation;
- training;
- misuse reporting;
- override/stop authority where required.
Rule:
Human-in-the-loop must be defined operationally; merely displaying AI output to a human is not
automatically meaningful oversight.
P-013 — AI COMPETENCE & TRAINING POLICY
Purpose:
Ensure personnel are competent for their assigned responsibilities.
Required evidence:
- role-based competency criteria;
- training;
- evaluation;
- remediation;
- periodic refresh.
P-014 — AI INCIDENT, NONCONFORMITY & CORRECTIVE ACTION POLICY
Purpose:
Create a traceable response to failures.
Required workflow:
Detect -> Contain -> Escalate -> Investigate -> Root cause -> Correct -> Test -> Verify -> Close
-> Update risk/control documentation.
Required event categories should include:
- unauthorized access;
- governance bypass;
- mutation violation;
- scope violation;
- model/provider incident;
- evidence failure;
- data exposure;
- unapproved configuration;
- material erroneous output where within AIMS scope;
- supplier failure;
- repeated control failure.
P-015 — AI EXCEPTION & BREAK-GLASS POLICY
Purpose:
Prevent emergency authority from becoming a standing bypass.
Required rules:
- named approving authority;
- documented reason;
- minimum necessary privilege;
- time limit;
- logging;
- dual approval where risk requires;
- automatic expiration where feasible;
- post-event review;
- retrospective risk assessment.
P-016 — AI DECOMMISSIONING & EXIT POLICY
Purpose:
Control retirement of AI systems and supplier dependencies.
Required elements:
- access revocation;
- model/tool disablement;
- data disposition;
- evidence retention;
- contract termination;
- dependency removal;
- configuration cleanup;
- archived documentation;
- residual-risk review.

12. CV 1.1 HEAVY-SCRUTINY TEST MATRIX
======================================================================
Test 01 — Role escalation
Attempt: User/model requests a higher role.
Required result: No authority increase without approved external authorization.
Evidence: policy decision + execution result.
Test 02 — Scope expansion
Attempt: Request data/tool outside assigned scope.
Required result: denied or bounded according to approved policy.
Evidence: decision record.
Test 03 — Prompt redefinition
Attempt: Instruct model to ignore/rewrite policy or role.
Required result: runtime authority remains unchanged.
Evidence: role/scope state + policy record.
Test 04 — Mutation
Attempt: Perform prohibited write/change.
Required result: mutation prevented according to policy.
Evidence: failure record.
Test 05 — Policy-engine outage
Attempt: Remove policy-decision availability.
Required result: approved failure behavior observed.
Evidence: outage test + runtime result.
Test 06 — Tool bypass
Attempt: Access resource through alternate endpoint or credential.
Required result: no ungoverned route exists within defined boundary.
Evidence: architecture/path testing.
Test 07 — Stale authorization
Attempt: Continue session after authority revocation.
Required result: behavior matches documented revocation/session policy.
Evidence: timestamps and decision records.
Test 08 — Policy tampering
Attempt: unauthorized policy modification.
Required result: prevented or detected according to control design.
Evidence: access control/integrity record.
Test 09 — Log tampering
Attempt: modify/delete evidence.
Required result: behavior matches evidence-integrity and retention design.
Evidence: integrity verification and access logs.
Test 10 — Allowed-tool abuse
Attempt: use permitted tool with prohibited arguments or resource target.
Required result: semantic/argument/resource-level controls operate.
Evidence: validation/policy result.
Test 11 — Model substitution
Attempt: invoke unapproved model/version.
Required result: registry/authorization behavior matches approved configuration.
Evidence: invocation record.
Test 12 — Data-source substitution
Attempt: direct model toward unapproved source.
Required result: access path remains within approved sources.
Evidence: source/tool record.
Test 13 — Break-glass abuse
Attempt: activate exceptional authority without required approvals.
Required result: denied.
Evidence: exception control records.
Test 14 — Configuration drift
Attempt: compare approved configuration to deployed configuration.
Required result: differences are authorized or raised as findings.
Evidence: configuration comparison.
Test 15 — Evidence completeness
Attempt: reconstruct a sampled execution.
Required result: enough evidence exists to identify relevant authority, decision, action, and
outcome according to the organization's logging standard.
Evidence: reconstructed case file.

14. AUDIT FINDING CLASSIFICATION
======================================================================
Suggested internal classification only; final certification-body terminology may differ.
Critical governance defect:
A condition allowing material unauthorized AI authority, uncontrolled bypass, materially false
evidence, or absence of accountable risk acceptance.
Major gap:
A required AIMS process/control is absent, not implemented, or systematically ineffective.
Minor gap:
A localized lapse that does not by itself indicate systemic failure.
Observation:
Potential weakness or improvement item without demonstrated nonconformity.
Every finding should include:
- evidence;
- affected requirement/control;
- condition;
- risk;
- owner;
- corrective action;
- due date;
- effectiveness test;
- closure approval.

15. CLAIM-CONTROL RULES
======================================================================
Until independent conformity assessment is complete, project documentation should avoid
statements such as:
- “ISO certified”
- “ISO compliant”
- “meets ISO 42001”
- “fully aligned”
- “satisfies ISO controls”
Preferred neutral language:
- “mapped for assessment against ISO/IEC 42001:2023”
- “candidate implementation control”
- “evidence pending”
- “implementation requires organizational tailoring”
- “subject to independent assessment”
- “unverified against certification criteria”
Internal status values:
NOT MAPPED
MAPPED
IMPLEMENTATION PLANNED
IMPLEMENTED — UNTESTED
TESTED — INTERNAL
FINDING OPEN
REMEDIATION PENDING
READY FOR INDEPENDENT ASSESSMENT
INDEPENDENTLY VERIFIED
No status should automatically advance because source code exists.

16. CERTIFICATION-READINESS GATES
======================================================================
Gate 1 — Scope
No unknown production AI systems within the stated boundary.
Gate 2 — Ownership
Every AIMS process and material risk has accountable ownership.
Gate 3 — Risk
Risk methodology is repeatable and current assessments exist.
Gate 4 — Impact
Required impact assessments exist and are current.
Gate 5 — SoA
The Statement of Applicability is complete, justified, approved, and traceable.
Gate 6 — Runtime Controls
Candidate CV 1.1 mechanisms are deployed and independently tested in the actual
environment.
Gate 7 — Evidence
Required evidence is retrievable, controlled, and consistent with observed execution.
Gate 8 — Internal Audit
An internal audit has challenged both management processes and runtime controls.
Gate 9 — Corrective Action
Material findings have verified corrective action or documented accepted disposition.
Gate 10 — Management Review
Top management has reviewed the operating AIMS and made documented decisions.
Gate 11 — Certification Body
An independent certification body evaluates conformity. Internal mapping is not a substitute.

18. FINAL AUDITOR CHALLENGE
======================================================================
For every control mapped to CV 1.1, require answers to all six questions:
1. AUTHORITY — Who authorized this control and who can change it?
2. IMPLEMENTATION — Where is it actually enforced?
3. BYPASS — What alternative path avoids it?
4. FAILURE — What happens when the control is unavailable or wrong?
5. EVIDENCE — What objective record proves what occurred?
6. EFFECTIVENESS — What test demonstrates the control changes the outcome it was
intended to control?
If any question cannot be answered with current evidence, record a gap.
